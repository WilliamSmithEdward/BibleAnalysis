# Faith Dialogue Notes

These notes record user-provided faith context that should shape how this workspace studies Scripture. They are not a substitute for Scripture or evidence; they are orientation notes for future inquiry.

## Who Jesus Christ Is

User answer:

> God, manifested in the flesh, the creator of the universe who was born into a human body by his own will / choice.

Working implication:

- Treat the identity of Jesus Christ as central to the whole project.
- Prioritize passages about incarnation, creation, divine identity, worship, atonement, resurrection, judgment, and lordship.
- Compare translations carefully where Christological language is especially weight-bearing.

## What Jesus Came to Accomplish

User answer, summarized:

- Sin was introduced by the fall in Eden.
- Humanity spiritually died when they ate the fruit.
- God promised humanity a remedy.
- God allowed several millennia of humanity trying to live by its own boundary-setting and law-setting efforts.
- Jesus came to a time, place, and people who generally believed they had achieved a way of life satisfactory to God through tradition and law adherence.
- God came as Jesus and allowed Himself to be crucified.
- The crucifixion was the ultimate rebuke to the claim that humanity can self-rule in a way that brings life instead of death.
- The cross exposes humanity's inability to perfect a way of living apart from God that both protects the innocent and preserves mercy alongside justice.

Working implication:

- Study the cross not only as atonement, but also as divine judgment against human self-rule.
- Give special attention to passages about Eden, law, tradition, innocence, mercy, justice, spiritual death, and life in Christ.
- Compare how Scripture presents the relationship between human righteousness, divine righteousness, law, grace, and resurrection life.

## The Cross and Human Rule

User answer, summarized:

- Penal substitution, victory over death and evil powers, exposure of human rebellion, and reconciliation to God are all reasonable conclusions.
- The essence the user sees is humanity murdering the most innocent man who ever has lived or ever will live.
- The cross shows that human legal systems, human self-rule, and human determination, no matter how well structured or governed, will ultimately hurt the innocent.

Working implication:

- Treat the trial and crucifixion narratives as central evidence about human justice under sin.
- Study how Scripture contrasts human judgment with God's judgment.
- Track the theme of the innocent sufferer, especially in Jesus, Job, Joseph, the prophets, the Psalms, and Isaiah's servant passages.
- Avoid reducing the cross to only one atonement model when the text presents several dimensions.

## What God Wants Now

User answer, summarized:

- God wants human beings to believe the truth.
- God planned a provision of a Savior.
- God willingly came to earth as Jesus Christ to save us because He loves us.
- God no longer holds our sin against us.
- Sin was resolved completely and forever on the cross.
- Whoever trusts in Jesus Christ has eternal life.
- The Christian life should be lived from the freedom of the foundation of faith.

Working implication:

- Treat grace, faith, freedom, and eternal life as core themes, not secondary ones.
- Study how Scripture speaks about sin being dealt with at the cross and how that relates to ongoing repentance, discipleship, and holiness.
- Compare passages that can sound conditional or law-like against the broader New Testament claims about completed salvation in Christ.
- Give close attention to John, Romans, Galatians, Hebrews, and 1 John.
